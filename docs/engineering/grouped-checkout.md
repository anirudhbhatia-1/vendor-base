# Phase 7 grouped checkout

Phase 7 is implemented for the approved company-local, synthetic-data environment. It converts one authenticated customer's validated grouped cart into one checkout group, one prepaid synthetic payment authorization, and exactly one independent order for every quoted vendor location.

## Transaction boundary

`GroupedCheckoutService.createQuote()` revalidates ownership, delivery address, active vendor/location/publication state, service area, current product/variant/price versions, store minimum, and counted inventory. It calculates INR amounts only in integer paise, under the `finance.financial_policy` version in force at the quote's own instant (`resolveFinancialPolicy(at: now)`), whose reference — `SYNTH-POLICY-FIN-V<n>-<row head>` — is stamped on the quote and carried onto every payment allocation, so the order settles under the rules it was sold under. A five-minute quote stores immutable group and line snapshots and creates row-locked per-line inventory holds. The quote is bound to the cart's content by `quoteContentDigest` — customer, cart, address, each group's method and tip, each line's variant, quantity, price and catalogue versions, never the stock on the shelf — so a retry with the same content replays the live quote, and any change re-quotes: the cart's earlier live quotes are retired as `INVALID` and its lapsed ones as `EXPIRED`, their holds given back first, so a customer is never blocked by their own earlier hold. Until audit run 43 the digest hashed the shelf too, so the first quote's own hold broke every retry, and a cart-only shortcut returned whatever live quote existed — the one the customer had just changed away from. Every active group is returned, quoted or blocked with a reason (`STORE_UNAVAILABLE`, `FULFILMENT_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE`, `LINE_UNAVAILABLE`, `MINIMUM_NOT_MET`, `INVENTORY_UNAVAILABLE`, in the order the customer can repair them); inner joins used to drop a closed, paused or out-of-zone store from the quote entirely and quote a group *short* of any line without a current price or stock record. A declined or failed payment and an expired quote release their holds through the inventory module's routine, which restores the units, recomputes the shelf state and records the release. Invalid sibling groups are returned with an explicit reason and do not consume stock. The blocked reasons are `FULFILMENT_METHOD_UNAVAILABLE` (the store no longer offers the group's method — checked first, because the customer can repair it without changing what they are buying), `MINIMUM_NOT_MET` and `INVENTORY_UNAVAILABLE`. A `CUSTOMER_PICKUP` group is priced with no delivery fee; delivery groups carry the distance fee. Before batch 10 the pricing ignored the method and charged every group the distance fee, collection included.

`place()` owns checkout idempotency. The customer and quote are rechecked, the customer's lapsed quotes are released, current catalogue/location versions must still match, the cart's content digest must still equal the quote's (a line added, a quantity, a method or the address changed since the quote is refused as `Quote contents changed`), and a repeated quote or customer idempotency key returns the original checkout. A successful authorization creates the checkout, vendor orders, order-line snapshots, provisional reservations, exact payment allocations, aggregate transitions, checkout/payment/inventory history, and transactional outbox events in one PostgreSQL transaction.

Declines commit the release of only that quote's reservations and create no order. Timeout, network-equivalent, or malformed provider outcomes are stored as `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN`; the client is told not to retry payment. A later verified webhook can finalize or cancel the same checkout.

## Provider boundary

`MoneyProviderAdapter` separates authorization, capture, void, refund, payout and status operations from checkout. Every operation is asynchronous, because a real provider is a network away, and every answer is one of `SUCCEEDED`, `DECLINED` or `UNKNOWN`; a provider that throws — a malformed response, a timeout, a dropped connection — is turned into `UNKNOWN` by `providerOutcome` at every call site, never into a decline and never into an error that rolls the in-flight record back. `LocalFakeMoneyProvider` supplies deterministic `SUCCESS`, `DECLINE`, `UNKNOWN`, and `MALFORMED` company-test scenarios. It is synthetic by design and must be replaced through the production replacement register before any real-money environment.

The local webhook endpoint verifies the HMAC-SHA256 signature over the exact JSON payload, a five-minute timestamp window, provider event shape, provider operation ownership, and unique event identity; an outcome other than `SUCCEEDED` or `FAILED` is refused at the boundary rather than read as a failure. The receipt is the replay guard and is taken under the payment's row lock, so two deliveries of one event are received once and replayed once; a reused event id carrying different content is refused as a conflict. Replayed events return a replay result without duplicating orders, allocations, transitions, or outbox records.

### A placement the provider did not answer (7.4)

A placement whose authorization came back `UNKNOWN` is held as `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN` with a `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN` finance exception opened at once (`AWAIT_PROVIDER_WEBHOOK`). Its stock holds stand past the quote's own life — the expiry sweep leaves a quote alone while its group is `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN` — because a late "succeeded" needs them. After `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN_DEADLINE_MS` (fifteen minutes) the worker's `abandonUnknownPayments` sweep gives the stock back, retires the quote, cancels the group (`PAYMENT_ABANDONED` event, `checkout.payment_abandoned` outbox event) and re-labels the exception `RECONCILE_ABANDONED_AUTHORIZATION`; the payment stays `UNKNOWN_REQUIRES_RECONCILIATION`, because it is.

A verified webhook that arrives while the group is `PAYMENT_UNKNOWN` finalizes or cancels it as before and resolves the exception. One that arrives later is handled by what it says about the money: `SUCCEEDED` for an abandoned payment is recorded as `AUTHORIZED` and voided through the provider at once (`VOIDED`, exception resolved) or, if the void goes unanswered, left `AUTHORIZED` with the exception asking for `VOID_LATE_AUTHORIZATION`; `FAILED` for an abandoned payment closes it; an answer that contradicts a payment the checkout already settled (a failure for an authorized order, a success for a declined one) opens a `STATUS_MISMATCH` exception for a person and moves nothing on its own.

## Customer API and projection

- `POST /customer/checkout/quotes`
- `POST /customer/checkout/groups`
- `GET /customer/checkout/groups/:checkout_group_id`
- `GET /customer/checkout/groups/:checkout_group_id/history`
- `POST /customer/checkout/provider-webhook` (signature-verified provider boundary; no customer capability)

Customer routes require `customer.checkout.manage` and conceal cross-customer identifiers. The group projection exposes only overall and child-order summaries. It derives aggregate status from child states and does not expose another store's product or finance details. History is cursor-based and carries request/correlation references.

The customer PWA loads the authenticated active cart, displays each quoted and blocked store, fulfilment method, promise, decision rule, amount breakdown, combined authorization, and the zero local buffer. It presents UPI, card, and wallet test choices, explicitly disables COD, and handles decline, unknown, partial blockage, success, and reconnect-safe result retrieval.

## Verification

The connected verifier covers a three-store cart with an unavailable sibling, the quoted amounts against the pricing module for each store's real route distance and the policy stamp against the version in force, quote retry (after the first quote's own hold has moved the stock), a changed cart re-quoting and retiring the earlier quote with its hold given back, a quote refused at placement once the cart moved on, a paused store returned as `STORE_UNAVAILABLE` and an unpriced line as `LINE_UNAVAILABLE` rather than dropped, checkout retry, exact order/allocation counts with the policy stamp carried onto the allocations, ownership concealment, malformed provider response (held unknown with its finance exception, resolved by the webhook), signed webhook success, webhook replay, a reused event id with different content refused, holds kept past the quote's life while the provider is awaited, abandonment at the deadline with stock returned, a late success voided through the provider and a late success whose void went unanswered left authorized with an exception, a late failure closing the payment, a contradicting answer opening a conflict without touching the order, two forced-concurrent deliveries received once and replayed once, and — after activating a successor policy an hour later — a quote for an earlier instant still priced under its own version while a later one takes the successor. `checkout-pricing.spec.ts` drives D-05's worked examples and boundaries as a table (the 2 km included distance, the 0.8-paise rounding at 2001 m, the 40 km boundary and its refusal, worked example 1 at ₹349, the fictional ₹1 tax over three lines); `financial-policy.spec.ts` proves the policy reader refuses any currency but INR, a missing or fractional field, and the absence of a policy. Existing inventory, money, cart, aggregate, messaging, and access verifiers cover row-lock contention, expiry/release, state-transition integrity, outbox integrity, and controlled finance behavior.
